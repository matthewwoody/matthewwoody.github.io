I also enjoy playing Go, although I haven't gotten to playing on the grand 19x19 board just yet. Passively, I'm a big time enjoyer of 90's and 2000's animated shows! If you're in the mood to pick up a new show, I absolutely recommend Batman: The Animated Series, followed by Batman Beyond; both of which are available for free on Internet Archive. Starting Off

I started out my high education journey as a business major- intending to never take another math class again! This mindset quickly collapsed during my first semester at Santa Rosa Junior College, where I was taking a finite math class and found it much more interesting than my business and econmics classes. The following semester, I switched my major to mathematics and went through the trials and tribulations of calculus, in addition to some computer science and physics.

Figuring It Out

I think the words of Charles De Mar from Better Off Dead more or less describe my sentiment during this time- "I've been at this high school for seven and a half years- and I'm no dummy!"
Essentially, after a couple semesters of classes and work at my part-time retail job, I began to crave something more for myself and started applying to some summer internships just to see what was out there. I ended up working for my local city government, helping optimize their water use billing system and catching some unnoticed backflows costing people money and the city water. I also helped out with their water conservation programs, spreading awareness about the rebates and benefits available to participants. It was a solid first internship experience and helped me realize I wanted to apply my technical knowledge to the environment. After that summer, I'd discovered that since I'd switched my major, I was off of the ideal community college transfer timeline and was left with a spring semester in which I could either take one more math class or try my hand at another internship, this time armed with more knowledge and desire to apply myself in Earth Science.

Off to Long Island

I ended up taking an internship offer at Brookhaven National Laboratory, doing radar data analysis on XSACR data from TRACER to isolate features of Houston's bay breeze. I developed Python workflows to extract leading edge position and propagation speed, generated time series statistics and compared frontal velocity with overall bay breeze wind speed. As a result of this, I realized how interested I was in atmospheric science, and wanted to continue working in the field. I tried to land some more internships for the summer, but many deadlines had passed and I thought I'd be sentenced back to retail for the summer. Thankfully, I was able to return to Brookhaven in a different role that I didn't even apply for! Basically, since I'd made a positive impression on the staff who organized the internship program, they invited me back to help coordinate the summer internship program. It ended up being a ton of fun, and I got to host social events and meet so many new people- but I left knowing that a research-oriented role was a better fit for me.

Back to School

Finally the time came for me to transfer to a 4-year school, and after having lived on my own since the start of the year, my heart wasn't in packing up and moving again to attend school, so I decided to stay in the neighborhood and go to my local state school. This was definitely the right choice for me, as I've enjoyed the smaller class sizes and relationships I've made with my professors- I'm definitely thriving at SSU.
